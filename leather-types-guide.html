A Guide to Leather Types

The leather decides how a bag looks, feels and ages. Here is what the common names on a label really mean.

I

Full-grain

Full-grain leather keeps the whole outer layer of the hide, with its natural grain and small marks intact. It is the strongest and most durable leather and develops a rich patina over the years. Expect some variation in texture: that is the sign of a genuine, untouched surface.

II

Top-grain

Top-grain leather has had its surface lightly sanded and finished to even out marks. It is a little thinner and more uniform than full-grain and resists stains well, but it develops less patina. Much fine, smooth handbag leather is top-grain.

III

Suede and nubuck

Both have a soft, velvety nap.

  • Suede comes from the inner side of the hide and is very soft but marks and absorbs water easily.
  • Nubuck is the outer grain, lightly buffed, so it is sturdier than suede.
  • Both need a suede brush and a protective spray.
IV

Saffiano and pebbled

Saffiano leather is pressed with a fine cross-hatch pattern and sealed with a wax finish, making it scratch-resistant and easy to wipe clean. Pebbled leather has a raised, rounded grain that hides scuffs and feels soft in the hand. Both are practical choices for everyday bags.

V

Patent and finishes

Patent leather has a high-gloss coating that repels water but can crack with sharp folds and transfer colour from other materials. Aniline leathers are dyed without a heavy topcoat for a natural look; semi-aniline adds a light protective finish. The less finish, the more the leather shows its character and wear.
